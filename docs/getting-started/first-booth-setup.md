# Set Up Your First Booth

A "booth" in Pix represents a single photobooth station. You'll create it in the dashboard, pair it with the Desktop App, then connect your camera and printer.

## 1. Create a Booth

Open the **Pix Dashboard** at pixture.io and:

1. Go to the **Booths** tab.
2. Tap **Create Booth**.
3. Give it a name (e.g., "Main Booth" or "Mall Kiosk 1").
4. Assign at least one template to the booth.

<!-- Screenshot: Create booth screen -->

## 2. Sign In on the Desktop App

1. Launch the **Pix Desktop App** on your booth computer.
2. Sign in with your Pix account credentials.
3. Select the booth you just created from the list.

The app downloads your assigned templates and configuration automatically.

## 3. Connect Your Camera

Pix works best with a **Canon EOS camera** connected via USB:

1. Turn on your Canon camera and connect it to your computer with a USB cable.
2. The Desktop App detects the camera automatically -- you'll see a green status indicator.

:::tip Webcam Fallback
No Canon camera handy? Pix can use your computer's built-in webcam for testing. This is great for trying out the session flow before you open to guests.
:::

:::caution
Make sure no other application (like Canon EOS Utility) is running, as it may lock the camera connection.
:::

## 4. Connect Your Printer

Optional, but recommended so guests can walk away with a physical print:

1. In the Desktop App, go to **Settings > Printer**.
2. Select your printer from the auto-discovered list and choose your paper size.
3. Run a **Test Print** to check colors and alignment.

Not printing at this location? Skip this step -- guests can still share via QR code. See [Printer Setup](/docs/desktop-app/printer-setup) for supported printers and full details.

## 5. Start a Test Session

Run a quick test to make sure everything works end to end:

1. Click the screen to start a session.
2. Select a template.
3. Take a photo and confirm it appears in the sharing screen (and prints, if you configured a printer).

If it looks good, you're all set!
