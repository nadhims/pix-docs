# Event Rental

With event rentals, the booth goes to the customer. A couple, a company or an agency books it for a wedding, a dinner or a product launch and pays you a flat fee; their guests use the booth for free. What sells the next booking is how the booth looks on the day, how smoothly it runs, and the album your client shares afterwards. This page shows how to set that up in Pixture.

## How It Makes Money

The client pays a fixed fee per event, usually agreed before the day. Weddings are the anchor for most operators, with planners, venues and agencies sending repeat work. Extras such as a second booth, video or slow-motion capture, or AI portraits can be sold into the package.

## The Setup

### 1. Create the event

Create an **Event** for each booking, with its dates, client and venue. An event runs itself: it is active from the moment you create it, the booths on it follow its dates, and it ends by itself the day after its last date. See [Run an Event](../tutorials/run-an-event.md).

### 2. License the event days

Every booth on an event needs Pix Pro or a **Day Pass** on its computer. If you only work weekends, Day Passes for the event days usually cost less than a subscription. Check **Licenses > Events** before the day: each event shows **Ready**, or which booths still need Pix Pro or a pass. See [Add a Second Computer or Move Pix Pro](../tutorials/add-or-move-a-computer.md).

### 3. Make it look like their event

- Give the event its own templates, from your own designs in Pix Design or one of Pixture's free template packs. See [Assigning Templates](../dashboard/pix-design/assigning-templates.md).
- Give it a kiosk look with the couple's names or the brand's colours. See [Customise the Kiosk Screens](../tutorials/customise-kiosk-screens.md).
- Switch the booths to video or slow-motion capture for the event if the client booked it. See [Event Video Modes](../desktop-app/event-video-modes.md).

### 4. Free for guests, or paid

A new event is **Free for guests**: booths on it skip the Payment screen. If the client wants guests to pay (a sponsor charging for keepsake prints, for example), give the event its own prices and, if the money should go to the client, its own payment gateway. See [Events](../dashboard/events.md).

### 5. The album and the report

With **Link Sharing** on, every guest gets their photos by QR code, and the event's **Album** collects every photo for your client to share. After the event, the **Report** tab shows how many sessions ran. See [Run an Event](../tutorials/run-an-event.md#step-6-share-the-album).

## On the Day

- Test the booth the day before with a free voucher, so you know payment, printing and sharing all work. See [Vouchers](../dashboard/vouchers.md).
- Set a **Menu PIN** so guests cannot reach the operator menu.
- Venue internet is often weak. The booth keeps shooting and printing offline and uploads when it can; bring a 4G router for the album and QR sharing. See [Offline Mode](../desktop-app/offline-mode.md).

## Typical Numbers

From the Pixture Photobooth Playbook (September 2026): at about US$700 a booking and US$220 of costs per event (attendant, prints, travel), three bookings a month bring roughly US$2,100, six about US$4,200 and ten about US$7,000. A locally built booth costs US$2,000 to 5,000 and pays back in a few months. These are estimates; your market sets the fee.
