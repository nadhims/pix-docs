# Set Up Flash and Lighting

In this tutorial you will pick the right kind of light for your booth, place it, connect a flash if you use one, and set the camera on the booth so a whole day of photos looks like one set. Allow about 30 minutes with the lights and a helper standing where customers will stand.

Light matters more than the camera. A mid-range camera with one good soft light beats an expensive body in a dark corner.

## When to Use It

- **A new booth or a new venue.** Mall corridors, wedding halls and cafes all have different light; set it up once per place.
- **An event rental** where you want the clean, sharp "studio" look of flash.
- **A self-service mall booth** that runs all day: continuous LED light is simple and never needs a recharge.
- **A pop-up box with a webcam:** use continuous light, since a webcam cannot fire a flash.

Not needed when the venue is already evenly bright and your test photos look right. If your only problem is a dark live view with flash, go straight to [Get a Bright Live View with Flash](./bright-live-view-with-flash.md).

## Before You Start

- Any plan: lighting works the same on Pix Starter, Pix Pro and an Event Pass.
- A camera connected and picked on the booth. See [Connect a Camera](./connect-a-camera.md).
- The lights: LED panels, a ring light or LED softboxes (continuous), or a speedlite or studio strobes with a wireless trigger (flash).
- Pixture Photobooth 1.2.37 or newer if you use flash with a Canon DSLR (for **EXTERNAL FLASH**).

## Prepare

### Continuous light or flash?

| | Continuous light (LED panel, ring light, LED softbox) | Flash (speedlite, studio strobe) |
|---|---|---|
| Works with | Camera bodies and webcams | Camera bodies only |
| Live view | Shows the photo as it will look | Can look dark until you fix it |
| Photos | Good, needs a bright light to keep ISO low | Brighter, sharper, freezes movement |
| Setup | Simplest | Needs a trigger and the right camera settings |
| Over a long day | Always ready | Needs recharge time between shots |

For a first booth, one or two bright LED softboxes are the easiest start. Move to flash once you are comfortable with manual camera settings.

### What to bring

- Light stands with sandbags. A stand knocked over by a guest ends the night.
- Diffusers: a softbox or an umbrella. A bare light or a pop-up flash gives hard shadows and squinting faces.
- For flash: a wireless trigger for the hot shoe and its receivers, spare batteries, and the flash's sync speed from the camera manual (usually 1/125 to 1/200).

## Step 1: Place the lights

1. Put one large soft light in front of the customers, slightly above eye level, pointing down a little. For most booths this one light is enough.
2. For groups, use two lights at about 45 degrees on each side, so faces at the edges are lit too.
3. If the backdrop should look clean, light it evenly from the sides, or move the customers about 1 metre away from it so their shadows fall on the floor.
4. Keep bright windows, lamps and LED screens behind the camera, not behind the customers.

## Step 2: Connect the flash (flash only)

The booth takes a photo by pressing the camera's shutter for you. Whatever fires when you press the shutter by hand fires from the booth too: the pop-up flash, a speedlite on the hot shoe, or strobes on a wireless trigger.

1. Put the trigger on the camera's hot shoe and set the strobes to the trigger's channel.
2. Set the flash to **manual power** (not TTL), around half power for a start, so every photo comes out the same.
3. Turn the camera's live view on and press the shutter by hand. The flash should fire.

<!-- SHOT-NEEDED: hardware: camera with a wireless trigger on the hot shoe and a strobe in a softbox -->

If the flash fires by hand but not from the booth, and the camera is a Canon DSLR, turn on **EXTERNAL FLASH** in Step 3.

## Step 3: Set the camera on the booth

1. Open the operator menu and tap **Camera Settings**.
2. Set **ISO**: 100 to 200 with flash or strong continuous light, 400 to 800 with weaker light.
3. Set **SHUTTER**: with flash, at or below the sync speed (for example **1/125**). With continuous light, **1/125** or faster so moving customers are not blurred.
4. Set **APERTURE**: f/5.6 to f/8 keeps a group sharp; f/2.8 to f/4 gives a softer background for one or two people.
5. Set **WHITE BALANCE**: **Flash** with strobes, or a fixed setting that matches your LED lights. Avoid Auto when the venue light changes over the day.
6. Tap **Save Camera Settings**.

![Camera Settings with ISO, SHUTTER, APERTURE, WHITE BALANCE and Save Camera Settings marked](/img/docs/steps/tutorials-flash-and-lighting--camera-exposure.webp)

The camera's mode dial must be on **M** for these to stick. A Canon DSLR with a non-Canon flash or a wireless trigger may not fire while live view runs: scroll to **CAPTURE** and turn on **EXTERNAL FLASH**, as shown in [Get a Bright Live View with Flash](./bright-live-view-with-flash.md#step-4-canon-dslr-only-turn-on-external-flash).

## Step 4: Fix a dark live view (flash only)

With flash settings the live view can look almost black, because the scene is dark until the flash fires. Customers then pose in front of a black screen. Add a modelling light, or turn off the camera's exposure preview: the steps for each camera brand are in [Get a Bright Live View with Flash](./bright-live-view-with-flash.md).

## Step 5: Take a test session

1. Tap **Back to Menu**, then **Start Booth**, and run a full session with your helper where customers will stand.
2. Check the photos on the review screen and the print. Too dark: raise the light or flash power, or open the aperture one stop. Too bright: lower the power.
3. Repeat with two or three people, to check the edges of a group.

## Check It Worked

- Faces are evenly lit, with no hard shadow on the backdrop.
- With flash, every photo of a session is lit, not only the first one.
- All photos of a session, and of the day, have the same brightness and colour.

## Tips

- **Some photos in a burst come out dark?** The flash had not recharged. Lengthen the countdown between shots in the Pix Design UI Editor (at an event: the event's **Edit appearance** > **Countdown**), or lower the flash power.
- **Speedlites on AA batteries slow down over a long event.** Studio strobes, or a speedlite with an external battery pack, recharge faster.
- **Glasses reflecting the light?** Raise the light higher, or move it further to the side.
- **A ring light alone gives flat light.** It is fine for close portraits, less so for groups.
- **Write your settings down** (ISO, shutter, aperture, power) per venue. The next event there takes two minutes.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The live view is black or very dark with flash | The camera previews the flash exposure | Modelling light, or exposure preview off: [Get a Bright Live View with Flash](./bright-live-view-with-flash.md) |
| The flash fires by hand but not from the booth | Canon DSLRs do not fire a non-Canon flash or trigger during live view | Turn on **EXTERNAL FLASH** under **CAPTURE** |
| A dark band across the photo | The shutter is faster than the flash sync speed | Set **SHUTTER** to 1/125 or 1/160 |
| Every other photo is dark | The flash is still recharging | Longer countdown between shots, or lower flash power |
| Photos are yellow or blue | White balance does not match the light | Set **WHITE BALANCE** to **Flash** or to your LED's setting |
| Photos look grainy | ISO too high for the light | More light, then lower **ISO** |
| People moving come out blurred | Shutter too slow under continuous light | **SHUTTER** 1/125 or faster, with more light |
