# Lighting & Flash

Good light matters more than the camera. A booth can use **continuous light** (always on) or **flash** (fires with the photo). Both work with the Pixture app.

## Does my flash fire with the booth?

Yes. With a camera body connected by USB (Canon, Sony, Nikon or Fujifilm), the Pixture app takes the photo by pressing the camera's shutter remotely, exactly like pressing the shutter button by hand. Whatever fires when you press the button fires from the booth too:

- The camera's **built-in pop-up flash** (raise it and leave flash on).
- A **speedlite** on the hot shoe.
- **Studio strobes or off-camera speedlites** fired by a **wireless flash trigger** on the hot shoe, or by a sync cable.

There is no flash setting in the app. Quick test: press the camera's shutter button by hand. If the flash fires, it fires from the booth.

A **webcam cannot fire a flash**. With a webcam, use continuous light.

## Continuous light or flash?

| | Continuous light (LED panel, ring light, LED softbox) | Flash (speedlite, studio strobe) |
|---|---|---|
| Works with | Camera bodies and webcams | Camera bodies only |
| Live view | Shows exactly what the photo will look like | Can look dark, see below |
| Photos | Good; needs a bright light to keep ISO low | Brighter, sharper, freezes movement |
| Setup | Simplest | Needs a trigger and the right camera settings |

For a first booth, one or two bright LED panels or an LED softbox is the easiest start. Flash gives the cleanest, most "studio" look once you are comfortable with it. The built-in pop-up flash works but is harsh and slow to recharge; use it for testing rather than events.

## Camera settings with flash

Put the camera in **M** mode and set exposure on the booth's [Camera Settings](./camera-settings.md) page:

- **Shutter speed** at or below the camera's flash sync speed, usually **1/125 to 1/200**. Faster than sync speed gives a dark band across the photo.
- **ISO** 100 to 200, **aperture** f/5.6 to f/8.
- **White balance** Flash.
- Set the flash to **manual power** rather than TTL for consistent results shot after shot, then adjust power until the test photo looks right.

**Recharge time:** a flash needs a moment to recharge between shots. If a burst of photos comes out with some shots dark, lengthen the countdown between shots in the Pix Design UI Editor, or lower the flash power. Speedlites on AA batteries slow down over a long event; studio strobes, or a speedlite with an external battery, recharge faster.

**Focus:** the booth fires the shutter without refocusing. Focus once on where guests stand, then switch the lens to **MF**.

## Live view looks dark with flash

At flash settings (low ISO, fast shutter) the scene is dark until the flash fires, and many cameras show that darkness in live view, so guests see a dark preview. Two fixes:

- Add a **modelling light** or a small continuous light so the preview is bright. Many studio strobes have a built-in modelling lamp.
- Or turn off exposure preview in the camera's menu, so live view stays bright regardless of the settings: **Exposure simulation** off on a Canon, **Live View Display: Setting Effect OFF** on a Sony, **Apply settings to live view** off on a Nikon Z, **Preview Exp./WB in Manual Mode** off on a Fujifilm.

## Placing the light

- One large soft light (softbox or umbrella) in front of the guests, slightly above eye level, is enough for most booths. Two lights at about 45° on each side give even light for groups.
- Keep bright windows and lamps behind the camera, not behind the guests.
- Light the backdrop evenly if it should look clean in the photo.
